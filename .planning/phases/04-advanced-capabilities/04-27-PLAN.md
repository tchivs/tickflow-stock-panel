## Policy-transition ownership and rule

- **Complete creation rule:** An immutable authorization revision is not itself evidence that a later rate-window charge belongs to that revision. At queue acquisition, the service records/loads current persisted revision B and passes its immutable ID into the existing acquisition transaction. That transaction joins the authorization's immutable revision A, requires `A == B` before creating a rate-window charge or queued/runnable job, and sources the charge revision from the authorization join only after that equality holds. A missing, inconsistent, or mismatched relation rejects through the established safe creation-denial/audit path with no consumption, job, work, or SSE; it never falls back to B or infers historical ownership.
- **Schema and transaction ownership:** Reuse the existing immutable `advanced_jobs → advanced_authorizations → advanced_policy_revisions` lineage and existing acquisition transaction. Do not add a mutable policy field, reset a window, merge revisions, migrate consumption, introduce another datastore, or create a second quota transaction.
- **Queued execution ownership:** For a job that was correctly acquired while A was current, `prepare_to_start()` records/loads current revision B, resolves the job's immutable authorization revision A, and rejects if their IDs differ before `quota_is_current()`, `_advance()`, workflow, provider, sandbox, runnable work, or SSE publication. It must neither inspect nor charge B's window on this branch.
- **API/lifecycle ownership:** Keep the existing FastAPI lifespan as deployment-policy source and the existing job route as safe projection boundary. Both transition-denied cursors expose one sanitized reason only; they never reveal policy IDs, fingerprints, quotas, or authorization details and never automatically reauthorize. A caller must obtain a new server authorization under B before retrying.

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|---|---|
| token authorization A to queue acquisition under policy B | An authorization is historical evidence, not authority to charge a later policy; the acquisition transaction must deny mismatch before consumption. |
| queued policy A to execution policy B | A correctly acquired historical charge remains immutable, while execution must obey current server-owned policy without opening or inspecting B's window. |
| job service to operational SQLite | Service decisions depend on immutable authorization/policy provenance and must not mutate or relabel existing accounting facts. |
| job lifecycle to workflow/SSE | A denied transition must stop before executable collaborators or scoped progress publication. |
